Class 1A · a mockup with made-up names

Two ways to make the times easy to read at one glance. In both, every lesson says its own start and end time, and an after-school lesson looks like any other lesson: no dashed edge and no "after school" words.

ASame shape as today, clearer times

  • Each lesson shows its time (8.00–9.00), so you do not look up to the top row.
  • A break is drawn only when a lesson comes after it that day: Tuesday of the Odd week has no Lunch band.
  • The top row counts in hours (8 am, 9, 10 …) with a strong line down the page at every hour, and it is repeated above the Even week.
  • Recess, lunch and assembly are striped bands with their names.

PHONEOn a phone: one day as a list

  • Opens on today. The buttons change the week and the day.
  • A computer and the printouts keep look A.

BDays across, time down the side

Odd weekEven week
  • Reads like a diary: time runs down the left side, one column a day, one week at a time.
  • A longer lesson is a taller box. Each box still shows its own time.
  • Wider boxes, so a band's subjects and its number of groups fit on one line.
